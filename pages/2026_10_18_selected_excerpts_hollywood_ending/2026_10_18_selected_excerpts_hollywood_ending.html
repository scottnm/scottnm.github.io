Selected Excerpts: Hollywood, Ending

[ 2026-10-18 ]

I read "Hollywood, Ending"

Here's a collection of things I highlighted or tagged in my first readthrough of the book.

Excerpts

Pt. 1: The Shoot - Juniper, Ch. 12, p. 77

Kai put his arm around me, and I felt really cared for in that moment. But of course, I did. I can't help but associate success with being loved; I was trained to do it.

Pt. 1: The Shoot - Juniper, Ch. 14, p. 87

You feel that certain relationships are so real, so deep, that they will last a lifetime [..]. It turns out the main reason you were friends is proximity, and having shared some strange intense experience together. In the rough seas of the real world, you can't get the magic back of being on a rooftop in Pittsburgh with someone you've finally accepted you love.

Pt. 2: The Test - Kai, Ch. 1, pp. 94-95

"You ever been sued?"
"No"
"Ever sued someone?"
I reached into my pocket and got a piece of gum. I like chewing gum—the old-fashioned stick kind. There's this flavor called Fresh Mint I've chewed since I was a kid, and it smells like a forest in the summertime. Ellen asked again if I'd ever sued anyone, and I said no, which was true, but I was pretty sure she knew. I could feel the electricity of it in the air. My chest felt tight.
I leaned backwards, hands across my chest. Among the many things I recommend about Fresh Mint gum is that when you're chewing it, you can't smell anything else.

Pt. 2: The Test - Kai, Ch. 11, p. 147

"You've already booked the lead in a huge movie, Juniper. And you're good in this. You really are."
"But don't you see the inadequacy of good? You don't get to make this your life by being good."

Pt. 3: The Junket - Kai, Ch. 6, p. 216

Maybe it doesn't matter who leaked it. Maybe it's like one of those Chinese dragons where a lot of people are under the dragon costume, some in the tail and some in the head and some in the middle. Maybe I don't need to know which person inside the dragon told the world, because really, the dragon did it.

Pt. 3: The Junket - Kai, Ch. 7, p. 224

I love listening to Juniper read. I've never been able to read out loud with any confidence or fluidity; Juniper reads like a professional narrator, with perfect enunciation and absolute surety. No word surprises her or trips her up. What must it be like, I wonder, to have that relationship with language, where everything flows, where it's no work to decode letters into sounds, and sounds into concepts.

Pt. 3: The Junket - Kai, Ch. 10, p. 236

I realize that Jill can write whatever she wants about my past, but she cannot write about my future. People always say that the present is the only moment that matters, but for me it's the future. When it⁂ was happening with him, I would always think about the future, how one day I'd be an adult. Now I think about Vancouver [...]. I do not actually have the present, this moment I don't control. What I have, what I will always have, is the perpetual future.

⁂ The "it" referenced here is Kai's abuse at the hands of a trusted teacher.

I found this section profoundly sad. It's sad in a way that I think from Kai's perspective is supposed to be hopeful—accepting what can't be changed and acknowledging his limited locus of control—but, it's heartbreaking to imagine a life where the present is never yours and you're perpetually in a future you'll never reach. Maybe its short-term reassuring, but it feels like long-term despair. It feels like not living. And it feels like an admission that this is what both his abuse and his job as an actor have taken from him.

Epilogue, pp. 290-291

That's the wonder of it all. You survive. It's a dirty lie that what doesn't kill you makes you stronger, or that everything happens for a reason, but this much is true: You survive.
The miracle of love is that Kai loved the Juniper he met in that Pittsburgh hotel, and he also loves this me. The miracle of love is that yes, I am here and you are there, but the door is ajar. We get through the door with empathy, with imagining, with acting. We play our way through it with music and dance and art. We break down the door with love. We love others until they can love themselves, until we can love ourselves. And all along the way, the future remains yours, ineluctably and irrevocably. You can cling to that future, as long as you need to, until with work and with time the future becomes present and the present past. You survive, and you survive, until one day, you survived.

Initial impressions

I quite enjoyed this book!

  • I found the romance earnest, sweet, and reassuring.
  • The plot of artists making art about another artist gave the characters a lot of time to wax poetic about the process of creation and purpose in a way that felt life-affirming to me. They weren't just characters with a MacGuffin to resolve, they had inner dimensionality and fears and wants and dreams that reminded me of the way I sometimes think to myself.
  • Kai's character arc as a survivor of sexual abuse has coincidentally fit into a lot of other things I've been reading these days, and I think helped reinforce a clearer picture for me about the victims of sexual violence and its consequences.
